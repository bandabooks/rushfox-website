When retailers think about security, they often picture a guard at the front door. Many losses, however, happen where customers never go: **receiving bays, back doors, storerooms and staff entrances**. Good access control closes these gaps.

## Know your entry points

Walk your premises and list every way in and out: customer doors, delivery bays, emergency exits, staff entrances, roof access and gates. Each one needs a clear rule for **who may use it, when, and how it is recorded**.

## Combine people and technology

- **Manned access control** at receiving bays: officers check delivery notes against goods and record vehicles and drivers in an access register.
- **Electronic access control** on staff and storeroom doors: card, tag or biometric readers record exactly who entered and when.
- **CCTV coverage** of entrances, receiving areas and cash points, so recorded events can be matched to video.
- **Exit checks** for staff and contractors, applied consistently and respectfully.

## Keep emergency exits compliant

Access control must never compromise safety. Emergency exits must still open for people evacuating, so the design must keep them both **secure from the outside and usable in an emergency**.

## Use the records

Access logs, occurrence books and footage are only useful if someone reviews them. Agree a reporting routine with your security provider, such as a weekly exceptions report covering forced doors, after-hours access and unusual deliveries.

## Handle personal information properly

Access records and footage contain personal information. Under POPIA, limit who can view them, decide how long they are kept and store them securely.
