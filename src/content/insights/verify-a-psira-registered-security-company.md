Every business that provides security services in South Africa must be registered with the **Private Security Industry Regulatory Authority (PSiRA)**. This includes guarding, patrols, reaction services and the installation of security equipment. The officers they put on your site must also be registered. Hiring an unregistered provider exposes you to poorly trained staff, weak accountability and the risk of no recourse if something goes wrong.

Here are five checks to make before you appoint a security provider.

## 1. Ask for the PSiRA business certificate

A registered security business holds a PSiRA certificate showing its **registration number**, the **date of issue** and a **valid-until date**. Current PSiRA digital certificates carry a QR code. Scan it, or check the certificate on the PSiRA website, to confirm it is genuine and still valid.

## 2. Check the officers, not only the company

Each security officer must be individually registered with PSiRA and graded (Grades E to A). Ask the provider how officers' registrations are checked before they are deployed, and ask to see the PSiRA cards of the officers assigned to your site.

## 3. Confirm employer registrations

A responsible security employer is registered for **UIF**, **COIDA** (Compensation Fund) and with the **National Bargaining Council for the Private Security Sector (NBCPSS)**. These registrations protect the officers working on your premises, and they protect you too.

## 4. For government and corporate tenders

Procurement teams usually also require:

- Company registration with CIPC
- A SARS tax compliance status PIN
- Registration on National Treasury's Central Supplier Database (CSD)
- A B-BBEE certificate or sworn affidavit

## 5. Get it in writing

Insist on a written proposal that states the number and grade of officers, shift hours, equipment, supervision and reporting. Vague quotations lead to vague service.
