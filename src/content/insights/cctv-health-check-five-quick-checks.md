A CCTV system is only useful if it records clear footage and you can find that footage when you need it. Too often a business learns, after a break-in, that a camera was offline, the hard drive was full or the night image was unusable. A short routine check prevents this.

## 1. Look at every camera, live

Open the live view on the recorder or the mobile app. Check that **every channel shows a picture**, that it shows the area it is meant to cover, and that nothing blocks the view, such as a new shelf, signage or a growing tree.

## 2. Play back last night

Pick a camera and play back footage from the early hours of the morning. This shows whether the **night-vision image is clear enough to identify a person**, and whether recording continued overnight.

## 3. Check the hard drive

In the recorder's settings, confirm the hard drive status shows as normal, not as an error or unformatted, and see **how many days of footage** it holds. If retention is shorter than your business needs, add storage or adjust the recording settings.

## 4. Confirm the date and time

Footage with the wrong date and time is hard to use as evidence. Make sure the recorder's clock is correct and set to update automatically.

## 5. Test your backup power

Power interruptions are exactly when criminals strike. Make sure your recorder, cameras and network switch are on a **UPS or battery backup** that keeps them running for the time you need.

## When to call a professional

Call a technician if a camera keeps going offline, the drive shows errors or images are blurry at night. A professional health check can also review camera positions, so that entrances, cash points and perimeters are covered with **identification-quality** images, not just general views.
